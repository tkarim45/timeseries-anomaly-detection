Time-Series Anomaly Detection — Architecture

A bench, not a plotting demo. It generates a series with KNOWN anomaly ground truth (point spikes, sustained level shifts, variance bursts at recorded indices), runs seven detectors, and scores each on precision, recall, F1 and point-adjusted F1.

Ground truth by construction

  • • the anomalies are injected at recorded indices, so every detector is scored against what actually happened rather than against another detector
  • • three anomaly shapes on purpose: point spikes, sustained level shifts, and variance bursts, because they fail different detectors
  • • seven detectors: rolling z-score, IQR, EWMA, STL residual, IsolationForest, and the two naive detectors re-run on the STL remainder

Point-adjusted F1, reported alongside pointwise

  • • point adjustment counts flagging ANY point inside a true anomalous segment as catching that segment
  • • a detector should not be punished for flagging a 30-step level shift one step late
  • • both pointwise and adjusted numbers are reported, so neither metric can flatter a detector on its own

The headline: seasonality is what breaks naive detectors

  • • on a series with a daily and weekly cycle the textbook rolling detectors COLLAPSE: raw IQR scores adjusted-F1 0.000 and raw rolling z-score 0.024
  • • the cause is the same for both: the seasonal swing widens the rolling fences (and dominates the rolling std) so nothing trips them
  • • strip the seasonality with STL and run the EXACT SAME detectors on the remainder: IQR goes 0.000 to 0.956, z-score 0.024 to 0.873
  • • and both beat IsolationForest (0.741) and STL's own remainder thresholding (0.716), so a simple detector on a properly deseasonalized signal beats a fancy detector on the raw one
